Designing for the beautifully messy real world

Interfaces should meet people where they are, not where a perfect diagram says they should be.

29.01.264 min read

Real life has interruptions, half-finished thoughts, different screen sizes, and days when the obvious route is not the available one. That is not edge-case territory. That is the territory.

Designing for people means leaving space for the unexpected. It means choosing language that helps instead of impresses, and building systems that recover gracefully when a plan changes.

Clarity is generous

A clear interface is an act of care. It reduces the amount of energy someone has to spend translating a tool before they can use it. The result is not less personality. It is more room for theirs.
